Criteria

Five tests for a product metaphor.

1It works at 20px.The nav is the hardest surface. A metaphor that holds there holds everywhere; the reverse is not true.
2It is not already a system icon.In the product, a folder with a lock is an action. A product icon should not read like one.
3It owns its main object.If two products share a folder, neither of them owns it.
4It names the product, not the category.A pie chart says analytics. Every product with a dashboard has one.
5It can become a 3D object.Marketing needs a hero it can light and render. A real object beats a UI glyph.

Behind the tests

Each test has a source in published research.

The more a target looks like its neighbours, the slower people find it.Duncan & Humphreys, 1989Test 3
Icons with their own mix of colour and shape are found before scanning starts. Shared features slow the search.Fleetwood & Byrne, 2006Tests 1 and 3
Icons are most distinct when they differ in meaning and in shape at once.McDougall, de Bruijn & Curry, 2000Test 4
Only home, print and the magnifier are close to universal. Giving a common glyph a second meaning creates conflict.Nielsen Norman Group, 2014Test 2
A close metaphor helps the first time. With use, familiarity takes over.Isherwood, McDougall & Curry, 2007The LDP trade-off

Abstraction that worked · Apple Photos

Apple traded a sunflower photo for a wheel of petals, and the category followed.

2012A photograph of a sunflowerApple's icon up to iOS 6. That year, in Apple v. Samsung, Susan Kare called it an arbitrary choice for the category.
2013iOS 7 turns it into a wheel of coloured petalsStill a flower, now abstract. The same mark went to the Mac in 2015 and is still there in 2025.
2015Google Photos launches with a pinwheelKept in its 2020 refresh. Samsung's Gallery draws petals too.

An abstract mark works when it shows up everywhere, the same way, for long enough to be learned. That is the plan for DXP and LDP.
